# Build it

These steps reach the first checkpoint: a modified vessel with the heaters installed and wired. Insulation, power and fan hookup, and gas handling come later.

**Tools:** MIG welder, drill and a step bit that goes up to 1", pliers, heat gun, wire strippers.

## 1. Re-gasket the lid

![Lid, gasket cement and rope gasket kit](images/lid-gasket-parts.jpg)

1. Pull out the stock silicone gasket. It won't survive the heat.

    ![Lid with the silicone gasket removed](images/lid-gasket-removed.jpg)

2. Run a generous bead of stove gasket cement around the lid's channel.

    ![Applying gasket cement in the lid channel](images/lid-gasket-cement.jpg)

3. Press in the rope gasket. I used a thin ribbon-style gasket from a hardware store, in two layers. The 3/8" rope in the parts list should also work; size it to your channel.
4. While the cement is still wet, put the lid on the fermenter and latch it. The gasket sets to the exact shape of the rim and seals airtight.

    ![Finished fiberglass gasket in the lid](images/lid-gasket-done.jpg)

## 2. Weld heater bungs into the grill grate

An old grill grate that sits at the top of the cone holds the heaters.

1. Use pliers to spread the grate bars where each bung will sit. Space the two evenly, a bit toward the outside.

    ![Spreading the grate bars with pliers](images/grate-spread-bars.jpg)

2. Set the bungs in place. **Check which way they face:** the bungs only thread from one side. Orient them so the heaters screw in from below and point up into the barrel.

    ![Two bungs sitting in the spread bars](images/grate-bungs-placed.jpg)

3. Weld them in. I used ordinary MIG wire. That isn't best practice on stainless, but this weld isn't critical.

    ![Bungs welded into the grate](images/grate-bungs-welded.jpg)

4. Screw the cartridge heaters into the bungs.

    ![Grate with both heaters screwed in, leads running into the cone](images/grate-heaters.jpg)

## 3. Replace the cone's side drain with a bung

1. Remove the small secondary drain valve on the face of the cone. Leave the main drain at the bottom.

    ![The small side drain on the cone](images/side-drain-original.jpg)
    ![Side drain removed](images/side-drain-removed.jpg)

2. Step-drill the hole out to fit the bung. A 1/2" NPT bung is about 1" across.

    ![Step-drilling the drain hole larger](images/side-drain-step-bit.jpg)

3. Insert the bung from inside, with the flange on the outside.

    ![Bung fitted through the cone](images/side-drain-bung-fitted.jpg)

4. Weld it from the outside. The bung is thick and the tank wall is very thin, so **lay the bead mostly on the bung and only just catch the tank**, or you'll blow through.
5. Seal over the weld with high-heat silicone so it's airtight. A sloppy weld is fine once it's sealed.

    ![The finished (ugly but sound) weld](images/side-drain-bung-welded.jpg)

## 4. Replace the lid's bubbler with a bung

Same process as step 3, on the center hole in the lid where the airlock was.

![Lid center hole before](images/lid-center-hole.jpg)
![Bung welded into the lid center](images/lid-bung-welded.jpg)

## 5. Add a drip hood over the side bung (optional)

The side bung sits at an angle on the cone, so liquid running down the inside can drip into it and leak out. Weld a small curved piece of sheet over the inside of the bung so liquid runs past it to the main drain.

![Inside the cone: the hood over the side bung sheds liquid to either side](images/drip-hood.jpg)

Welding this deep inside the tank is awkward. There's probably a better way. <!-- TODO: ideas? e.g. a threaded elbow pointing down, or positioning the bung higher. -->

## 6. Install the grate and heaters

1. Feed the heater leads down through the cone and out the side bung.

    ![Heater leads fed through the cone](images/grate-heaters.jpg)

2. Drop the grate into the tank so it sits at the top of the cone.

    ![Leads hanging out of the side bung](images/wires-out-side-bung.jpg)
    ![Looking in: the grate seated at the top of the cone, heaters standing up](images/grate-installed-top.jpg)

## 7. Splice onto high-temperature wire

1. Join **one lead from each heater** to one conductor of the 12 AWG mica wire, and the other lead from each heater to the other conductor. This puts the heaters in parallel.
2. Cover each splice with heat shrink.

    ![The two splices](images/splice-overview.jpg)
    ![Shrinking the splices with a heat gun](images/splice-heatshrink.jpg)

!!! note
    The mica wire is there to survive heat near the vessel. Check the heat-shrink rating too (see [Design → Electrical](design.md#electrical)).
